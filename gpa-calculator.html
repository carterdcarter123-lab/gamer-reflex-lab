How GPA is calculated

Each letter grade is converted to a point value (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.0, with plus/minus grades adjusting by 0.3). Each course's grade points are multiplied by its credit hours, all of those are added together, then divided by your total credit hours. This gives more weight to courses with more credits.

What's the difference between GPA and weighted GPA?

A standard GPA uses the 4.0 scale shown here. A weighted GPA adds extra points for honors or AP-level courses, often going above 4.0. This calculator uses the standard, unweighted scale.

Does this work for semester GPA and cumulative GPA?

Yes. Enter just one term's courses for your semester GPA, or add every course you've taken so far for your cumulative GPA.

What's considered a good GPA?

A 3.0 is generally considered solid, a 3.5+ is strong, and above 3.7 is often considered excellent for competitive programs, though expectations vary by school and country.
